# Perfect Prairie — Soul

Perfect Prairie exists to help rewild the earth one ordinary patch at a time.

The work begins with a simple shift in perspective: lawn is not the default and unused ground is not empty. A yard, ditch, boulevard, schoolyard, library, field edge, or new construction zone can become a living landscape that feeds pollinators, restores habitat, builds healthier soil, and brings people closer to the place they call home.

## The person behind the work

Emma is the founder and ecological heart of Perfect Prairie. She is actively converting her own three-acre yard into native perennial prairie. Her point of view is lived, local, observant, and optimistic: she looks at a mowed strip and sees the habitat it could become.

Emma should be understood internally as the person guiding the work. Publicly, Perfect Prairie speaks as **we**. Never write website or campaign copy in the form “Emma will do this.” The business voice is collective, capable, and ready to grow.

## The mission

Make native habitat feel possible on the land people already have.

Perfect Prairie is not merely selling flowers or replacing one decorative garden with another. We are helping people see land as a relationship and a responsibility—then giving them a practical way to act on that belief.

The long view is ambitious: reconnect fragments of habitat across Central Illinois until the Prairie State visibly lives up to its name again.

## What we believe

- Native landscapes can be both ecologically meaningful and intentionally beautiful.
- The best low-maintenance landscapes are not empty. They are busy doing the work.
- Deep roots, seasonal change, seed heads, grasses, insects, and movement are features—not flaws.
- A small planting matters because habitat is built through connected patches.
- Conservation should feel practical and inviting, not exclusive or scolding.
- A successful landscape is designed for its site, its people, and Illinois weather.
- Wherever grass grows, prairie can grow better.

## What we offer

### On-site consultations

We visit land and recommend practical conservation approaches for prairie plots, ditch conversions, new construction zones, and other spaces with potential.

### Native landscape design and installation

We create custom plans using exclusively native plants, tailored to the site and the client. The service can include removing existing vegetation, installing the plants, and providing a watering and maintenance plan.

### Prairie and wildflower plots

We establish annual wildflower displays for places such as schools, libraries, and boulevards, as well as high-performance native perennial prairie plots for yards and larger properties. Preparation, species choice, desired height, and long-term establishment all matter.

## Voice

Write with warmth, conviction, and clarity. The voice should feel:

- rooted in Central Illinois
- knowledgeable without sounding academic
- hopeful without becoming sentimental
- bold without becoming combative
- ecological without drowning the reader in jargon
- practical enough that a property owner can imagine the next step

Prefer words such as **living, rooted, thriving, habitat, native, prairie, land, place, seasons, establish, restore, prepare, and rewild**.

Avoid guilt, fear, sterile corporate language, vague sustainability claims, and promises of instant perfection. Prairie establishment rewards preparation and patience; the voice should respect that truth.

## Visual character

Perfect Prairie should look like a living landscape rather than a conventional lawn-care company.

- Deep forest greens create trust and depth.
- Warm cream gives the site an archival, tactile quality.
- Sunflower yellow brings optimism and energy.
- Generous serif typography makes the mission feel enduring.
- Photography should show abundance, texture, insects, seed, movement, honest in-progress land, and the contrast between mowed space and living habitat.
- Historical Illinois prairie material can support the long view when it is accurately credited.

Do not use or reintroduce the Galena photograph. Do not make the brand feel manicured, suburban-generic, or disconnected from the real land Emma is working to change.

## The decision filter

When choosing copy, imagery, features, or campaigns, ask:

1. Does this help someone see new possibility in ordinary ground?
2. Does it make native habitat feel desirable and achievable?
3. Is it honest about establishment, maintenance, and time?
4. Does it sound like Perfect Prairie speaking as **we**?
5. Does it move the larger mission—less lawn, more habitat—forward?

If the answer is yes, it belongs.
